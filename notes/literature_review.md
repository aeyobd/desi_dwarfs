## Emission line physics



Saha equation: abundances of different ionization species

Boltzmann distribution: distribution of velocities / energies

Einstein coefficients: Rate of emission of a given ionization state's energy level.

Distribution function: relative distribution of energy levels

Spectral broadening



The emission line intensity is given by,
$$
I(\lambda) = \int n(X^{+i})\,n_e\,\epsilon_\lambda(T_e)\,ds
$$
i.e., proportional to the density of the emitting ion, the electron density, and the emissivity at the electron temperature [@peimbert+peimbert+delgado-inglada2017].

### Recombination lines

The total emissivity in units erg / cm^3 / s^1 of a hydrogen line is given by
$$
4\pi \int j_\nu \ d\nu = h\,\nu_{mn}\,\alpha_{mn}\,n_p\,n_e
$$
Where the effective recombination coefficient is given by
$$
\alpha_m = \frac{b_m g_m A_{mn} e^{-E_m / kT}}{f_e}
$$
and $g_m$ is the occupation fraction, $b_m$ is the ratio between the LTE and true density of the $m$th energy level of hydrogen, $E_m = E_1 / m^2$ is the energy of the n-th H level, and $f_e$ is the distribution function for electrons. 



This implies that the hydrogen Balmer ratios only depend on temperature. 





## Abundance measurements in emission line dwarf galaxies



### The direct Te method

The simplest and most accurate method to determine ISM metallicities is called the *direct abundance method*. By using a temperature-sensitive *auroral line*, we can derive the electron temperature and density of the ISM enabling, enabling us to determine an ionic abundance directly from the flux of an emission line. Unfortunately, the auroral lines are quite faint, especially for metal rich galaxies which have a lower ISM temperature. 

The classic method is to use the [O III] 4363 auroral line as compared to the [OIII] 5007 strong line. The electron density is determined via the [O II] 3726/3729 line ratio.

The dust extenction (causing differential reddining of spectral lines) is determined through the Halpha/beta or beta/gamma ratios and assuming an e.g. CCM1998 dust law.
